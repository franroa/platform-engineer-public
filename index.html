Selected work

Six problems, and the paved roads
that made them stop hurting.

Patterns from the platform I model in the map — each one a problem teams kept hitting, the design that turned it into something self-service, and a link to the proof.

Developer experience

A new service ships through one merge request

Problem
Onboarding a service meant tickets, hand-offs and copy-pasted CI that drifted from every neighbour.
Approach
A golden path: three files in a template wire CI, cloud auth, state isolation and a namespace — all reviewed as an MR.
Result
Teams provision themselves; the guardrails are inherited, not remembered.
Read the write-up →
Multi-tenancy

Shared infrastructure, separate blast radius

Problem
Many teams on shared clusters — one team's mistake must never reach another team's data.
Approach
Per-tenant AD groups, resource groups, Key Vaults and namespaces from Terraform; RBAC as the fence.
Result
Multi-tenant efficiency without multi-tenant leakage — isolation you can audit, where a 403 is the design working.
Read the write-up →
AI platform

One governed front door to the models

Problem
Every team wanted LLM access, and each was re-solving auth, rate limits and routing on its own.
Approach
An AI gateway that authenticates callers, enforces per-tenant limits and routing, then forwards to the model backend.
Result
A single paved road to AI — governed, observable and per-tenant instead of a scatter of keys.

FinOps

The cost of a change is visible before it merges

Problem
Cloud spend was a monthly surprise — nobody saw the cost of a change until the invoice arrived.
Approach
Infracost prices every Terraform plan in the MR; OpenCost meters the running cluster by tenant and namespace — estimate and reality, side by side.
Result
Cost review moved from a monthly post-mortem to a number in the diff — the same shift-left move the safety gate made for risk.
Read the write-up →
Security

Infrastructure can't change unless policy says it may

Problem
A destructive change (a dropped database, a deleted network) was one wrong terraform apply away, caught only if someone noticed in review.
Approach
An OPA/rego gate evaluates every plan before apply; safety levels block destroys by data sensitivity, composed per cloud provider.
Result
Safety is a wall the change physically cannot pass, not a habit someone might forget — the safe choice is the one you get for free.
Open in the 3D map ↗
Resilience

The scariest failure is rehearsed, not feared

Problem
One shared gateway per cluster is deliberately a shared-fate zone — if it breaks, that's every tenant's outage at once.
Approach
A PodDisruptionBudget floors disruption, and Litmus kills gateway pods on a schedule while a continuous probe proves traffic keeps flowing — the hypothesis and its test, both in code.
Result
"Degraded but serving" is an engineered state, verified monthly — and a clickable failure-mode lens shows what every outage scenario actually takes down.
